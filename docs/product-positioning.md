# Product Positioning: Self-Hosted Meta Ads Agent

## Recommended Positioning

Sell this as a standalone, self-hosted Meta Ads agent inspired by the OpenClaw automation pattern, not as an official or required OpenClaw agent.

The product should feel like a packaged business tool that can be installed locally on a PC or deployed on a VPS. OpenClaw can remain part of the origin story and technical inspiration, but the buyer should not feel they must understand, trust, or install OpenClaw to get value.

## Core Message

Your own local Meta Ads operator. It watches campaigns every morning, kills obvious waste, flags winners, recommends budget moves, and asks before making risky changes.

## Suggested Product Description

A self-hosted AI agent for monitoring, pausing, and scaling Meta Ads with human approval. It runs on your own PC or VPS, connects to your Meta Ads account, generates a daily performance brief, auto-pauses clear losers, and stages bigger decisions for approval.

Inspired by the OpenClaw agent workflow, packaged as a standalone local/VPS install.

## Why Not Lead With OpenClaw

Leading with OpenClaw makes the product sound more like a developer template than a business-ready tool. It can also create unnecessary questions:

- Do I need to install OpenClaw?
- Is OpenClaw secure?
- Who maintains the dependency?
- Does the agent get broad system access?
- Is this a technical framework instead of a finished product?

The better trust angle is self-hosting, clear permissions, and human approval.

## Buyer-Facing Trust Points

- Runs locally or on the buyer's VPS.
- Meta credentials stay on the buyer's machine.
- Uses explicit Meta permissions for ads management.
- Auto-pause is limited to clear waste rules.
- Budget increases, resumes, and new campaigns require approval.
- Every action is logged.
- The dashboard shows what the agent sees and why it recommends changes.
- No agency retainer and no black-box SaaS controlling the account.

## Product Promise

The agent does not replace strategic thinking. It replaces the daily checking, obvious waste cleanup, and repetitive optimization workflow that busy advertisers often miss.

It should be sold as an operator and guardrail system:

- Daily monitoring
- Campaign health checks
- Fatigue detection
- Loser pausing
- Winner scaling recommendations
- Approval-based execution
- Exportable reports

## Naming Direction

Good positioning names:

- Self-Hosted Meta Ads Operator
- Meta Ads Agent OS
- AdPilot Local
- Meta Ads Control Room
- Local Ads Operator

Avoid names that make OpenClaw the primary brand unless selling specifically to developers already using OpenClaw.

## Recommended Marketing Angle

Lead with:

> Install your own Meta Ads operator in under an hour. It runs on your machine, checks your ads every morning, pauses obvious waste, and asks before spending more.

Support with:

> Inspired by OpenClaw-style agents, rebuilt as a standalone self-hosted product for marketers, agencies, and operators who want automation without giving up control.

## Packaging Implications

To support this positioning, the install experience should eventually include:

- One-command local setup.
- VPS setup guide.
- Meta OAuth or guided token configuration.
- Account selection.
- Dashboard launch.
- Telegram or email notification setup.
- A visible approval queue.
- `Con supervision` before enabling `Piloto automatico`.
- Clear permission explanation.
- Example campaign data for demo mode.

## Product Boundary

The product should not be presented as an official Meta tool, an official OpenClaw product, or a fully autonomous spend manager.

Use language like:

- "self-hosted"
- "approval-based"
- "operator assistant"
- "local/VPS install"
- "inspired by OpenClaw"
- "human-in-the-loop automation"

Avoid language like:

- "fully hands-off"
- "guaranteed ROAS"
- "official Meta automation"
- "OpenClaw required"
- "unlimited autonomous control"

## Final Position

This should be packaged and sold as a standalone self-hosted Meta Ads agent with an OpenClaw-inspired workflow.

That gives the product a stronger commercial identity, reduces perceived security risk, and makes it easier for non-technical buyers to understand what they are buying.
